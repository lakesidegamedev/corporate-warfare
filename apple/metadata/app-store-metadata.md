## Description (draft, matches `store/play/listing.md`)

Run a company in a living economy and grow it into a business empire. Corporate Warfare is an
offline corporate strategy game. Take over an established company or found your own, then build
plants, set prices, hire, borrow and expand across a map of the Great Lakes, Texas, Silicon
Valley or the Ruhr. Every market trades, every price moves, and every rival plays by the same
rules you do.

- Build and run divisions across fifteen industries, from farms and refineries to banks and
  software, with real supply chains between them.
- Watch the map come alive: your plants as buildings, freight on the lanes, traffic on the
  highways and weather drifting overhead.
- Trade stocks and commodities, each with its own price history.
- Fight rivals who undercut, invade and raid, or take them over.
- Ride the business cycle through booms, busts and historical commodity shocks.
- Play a career for a bronze, silver or gold medal over five, ten or twenty years, or keep going
  as a sandbox.

No account, no ads, no in-app purchases. The game plays fully offline. An anonymous market
summary is shared every five game weeks to balance the economy, and can be switched off in
Settings.

## What's New for 2.0 (draft; Apple allows 4,000 characters)

The new table layout is Windows and Linux only, so it is not claimed here. Owner: confirm the map
line against the shipped Apple build before submitting.

Corporate Warfare 2.0 gives your company a goal and an ending.

- Careers: take the helm of an established company or found your own. Pick a goal (richest,
  biggest, dominant or survivor) and play for bronze, silver or gold over five, ten or twenty
  years. Start without a goal for a sandbox.
- Rivals build, close losing plants and compete for room to grow. The Openings board shows where
  a new plant would pay.
- Raids are announced four weeks ahead, so you can raise your marketing and defend.
- A failed company's plants go to a four week auction. Bid for them, or list your own.
- World prices have weather, ports have limits, and demand follows the business cycle.
- Electricity is shared across a whole grid instead of one county.
- A livelier map, with smoke from running plants, traffic on the lanes and lights at night.

Saves from earlier versions still open and play on.
